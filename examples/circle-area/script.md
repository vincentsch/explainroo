# Why a circle has area pi r squared

## intro {chapter="Cut and rearrange"}
[#question] Why does a circle's area equal {πr²|pi times r squared}?
[#idea] Cutting it into slices helps us see where the formula comes from.

## measure {transition=fade}
[#radius] The radius, called {r|R}, measures from the center to the edge.
[#around] The circumference is the distance around, equal to {2πr|two times pi times R}.

## slices {transition=fade}
[#cut] Cut the circle into equal slices, each meeting at the center.
[#halves] Color half the slices yellow, and the other half blue.

## arrange {transition=cut}
[#move] Lay the slices side by side, alternating their directions.
[#same] Moving these pieces keeps their total area the same.

## refine {chapter="Approach a rectangle" transition=fade}
[#more] With more, thinner slices, the curved edges become flatter.
[#limit] As the slices get arbitrarily thin, the shape approaches a rectangle.

## edges {transition=fade}
[#top] Half the circle's circumference runs along the top, and half along the bottom.
[#width] Each long side approaches {πr|pi times R}, half of {2πr|two times pi times R}.

## height {transition=fade}
[#radius] Each slice has straight sides of length {r|R}, the circle's radius.
[#height] As the slices narrow, the rectangle's height approaches {r|R}.

## example {chapter="Multiply the two sides"}
[#two] For a radius of two, the rectangle's width is {2π|two times pi}.
[#multiply] Its height is two, giving an area of {4π|four times pi} square units.

## conclusion {hold=1.8}
[#product] A rectangle's area is its width times its height.
[#answer] So the circle's area is {πr²|pi times r squared}.
