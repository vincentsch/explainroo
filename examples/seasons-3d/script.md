# Why we have seasons

## hook {chapter="The question"}
Earth is closest to the Sun in early January. [#cold] That is the middle of winter in the north. So distance can't explain the seasons.

## distance
Over a year, the distance changes by about three percent, [#range] from 147 to 152 million kilometers. That is too little to make summer and winter.

## tilt {chapter="The tilt"}
The real reason is a tilt. [#axis] Earth's axis leans about 23 degrees, [#fixed] and it points the same way all year.

## orbit
As Earth goes around the Sun, [#june] first the north leans toward it. [#december] Six months later, the south does.

## light {chapter="What the tilt does"}
Steep light lands on a small area and heats it well. [#slant] The same light at a low angle spreads out, [#spread] so each square meter gets less.

## days
The half that leans toward the Sun also has longer days. [#hours] More hours of steep light make summer.

## outro {hold=1.5}
So the seasons come from the tilt, not the distance. [#both] When it is summer in the north, it is winter in the south.
